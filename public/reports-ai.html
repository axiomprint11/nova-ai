Revenue by product

How to read this report — and what it can tell you about pricing
Top sellers — the Revenue / Units / Orders switch on the chart and the same columns in the table. A product high on Orders but low on Revenue is a traffic product (keep it cheap, it brings people in); high on Revenue with few orders is a margin product.
Cooling down / heating up — every figure is compared with the period of the same length right before it (the arrows). A product whose orders fall while its Per unit price rose is price-sensitive — the last increase cost volume. Falling orders at the same price point to demand, competition or the website, not price.
Brings new clients — New under Clients counts customers whose first ever order with us was this product. Those are the products to advertise and to keep competitively priced; raise prices on products that mostly serve existing clients (reorders, few new).
Room to raise the price — a high Reorders share with a strong Quotes → orders conversion and rising orders: customers accept the price. Test a small increase there first. A low conversion (many quotes, few orders) says the price or the turnaround scares people off — look at the price ladder before cutting.
Express share — customers paying the rush fee shows how much they value speed; a product with a high express share can carry a faster standard turnaround at a higher base price.
Production days — median (typical) and average from the first production scan to the "complete" scan; Order → ready from the order itself (prepress and proofing included). The longest ones are where capacity, outsourcing or a price for the labour should be looked at; the shortest are the ones to promote as quick turnaround. On time = ready by the job's complete-by date.
